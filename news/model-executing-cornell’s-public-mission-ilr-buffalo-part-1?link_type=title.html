Who/What is the Buffalo Co-Lab?

Cornell’s history is a unique one, and the university’s imprint reaches far beyond Ithaca’s East Hill. These engagements, however, have mostly been long erased from the minds of most Cornellians. This includes Cornell’s complicity in racial injustice, including having amassed wealth via seized indigenous land in New York State and beyond. As the largest employer in Tompkins County, Cornell’s economic impact is also felt in wage levels, housing prices, and today even pandemic management. So, as universities across the country attempt to bridge town-gown divides, the work of fulfilling our public mission is one of the key ways to atone for the deep scars left bare by the Ivy tower.

The ILR School’s Co-Lab in Buffalo has been doing the work of fulfilling Cornell’s public mission for 75 years, today under the new leadership of Catherine Creighton, and in the long fierce shadow of previous directors Lou Jean Fleron and Lois Gray. As the main center of excellence for Cornell in Western New York, the ILR Buffalo Co-Lab has focused on advancing the rights and power of workers through partnerships with public officials, unions, community organizations, and advocates. For example, the Partnership for the Public Good (in part incubated by the Buffalo Co-Lab), continues to work closely with the office, most recently in the thick of the city’s robust mutual aid efforts during the pandemic.

The Buffalo Co-Lab is many things, including a research hub, a site for training students and practitioners, engaging the labor movement, and disseminating research to the public. The rich ILR-connected civic, economic, cultural, and educational network in Buffalo has promoted innovative university-community learning for decades, with the goal of fulfilling ILR’s land-grant mission to improve work and economic vitality.
